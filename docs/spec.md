# Seriously Simple Volunteer Scheduler (SSVS): product spec

Status: draft, October 2026. Owner: benny Vasquez.

This spec describes what the app does and why. It's written for anyone building or deploying it. Each organization keeps its own setup notes outside this repository; [deployments/README.md](deployments/README.md) says what they cover.

## 1. The problem

Volunteer-run organizations keep a shared calendar, a chat channel, and a lot of goodwill. When someone can't make their shift, they post in chat, text a few people, or tell the coordinator, who then chases replacements by hand. Nobody can see at a glance which shifts are open, people miss the message in whichever channel it landed, and the coordinator becomes the bottleneck for every swap.

## 2. Goals

- A volunteer can release a shift or take an open one in under a minute, from a phone, starting from a link in an email, text, or chat message.
- Open shifts get filled without a coordinator sending messages by hand.
- A coordinator can see every gap for the week on one screen.
- New volunteers can find and sign up for training (shadow) shifts on their own.
- Small organizations can run it on a modest Linux server without paid services.

## 3. Non-goals

- Membership management, payments, or donor tracking. The app reads a member list from another system instead (section 11).
- Chat. The app posts to chat; it doesn't replace it.
- Hosting many organizations in one install.

## 4. UX principles

1. **Phone first.** Most actions start from a link in a message and happen on a phone.
2. **One question per decision.** When a choice matters (one date or every week?), the app asks it plainly and doesn't preselect an answer.
3. **Plain language.** "Your Thursday front desk shift", never "assignment instance". No internal terms on volunteer screens.
4. **Lists over grids on a phone.** A volunteer's home screen is "my next shifts" and "open shifts I can take". Phones get a list by day for everyone. On a computer, volunteers see the same read-only week grid coordinators use, so everyone is looking at the same schedule.
5. **Accessible as built.** WCAG 2.1 AA, real buttons and form labels, touch targets of at least 44 pixels, and color never the only signal.
6. **Test before building.** Each new flow gets tried as a clickable prototype with three to five real volunteers before backend work starts.

## 5. Concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organization | The one group this install serves. Has a name, a time zone, and settings. |
| Area | A place or kind of work that needs staff, such as Front desk, Hosted events, or Outreach events. Every shift belongs to one area. |
| Event | Optional. Something with a date or date range, a location, a description, and an organizer, such as a festival booth. An event holds one or more shifts. Events are where event planning happens, and they bridge the gap between event volunteering and regular shifts. |
| Weekly slot | A repeating shift template: area, weekday, start and end time, how many people it needs, how many shadow spots it offers, an optional required skill, and whether it repeats every week or every other week. |
| Shift | One dated occurrence. Generated from a weekly slot, or created on its own (one-time shifts and event shifts). |
| Regular | A person attached to a weekly slot, with a start date, an optional end date, and whether they work every week or every other week (with an anchor date). Regulars are filled into each generated shift they cover automatically. |
| Assignment | A person on a specific shift. Type is regular or shadow. |
| Shadow spot | An optional extra place on a shift for someone in training. It never counts toward the shift's people needed and never triggers cover requests. Volunteers who aren't cleared sign up for one only on a shift with at least one regular on it, so there's someone to learn from. |
| Skill | A qualification a lead grants, such as "Blacksmithing demos". A shift can require one. |
| Closure | A date or date range when an area is closed. Cancels shifts on those dates. |
| Cleared | A flag on a volunteer meaning they can take regular shifts and get cover requests. Off until a coordinator clears them, they're given a role, or the people import clears them by category (`--cleared-tag`), and a coordinator can move them back to shadowing. |

## 6. Roles and permissions

| Role | Scope | Can do |
|---|---|---|
| Admin | Everything | Manage the install, settings, integrations, and all users. |
| Coordinator | All areas | Invite and remove volunteers, clear them or move them back to shadowing, create areas, events, slots and shifts, assign anyone, add closures, set how many shadow shifts come before review. |
| Area lead | One or more areas | Create and edit slots and shifts in their areas, assign people, add shadow spots, grant skills, mark shadows' no-shows. |
| Event organizer | One event | Edit that event's shifts and see its roster. |
| Volunteer | Themselves | See the full schedule and who's on each shift (read-only), take open shifts (once cleared), release their own shifts, sign up for shadow spots (until cleared), manage notification settings. |

One person can hold several roles. Nobody sees schedule details until a coordinator has invited them and they've accepted. Each area can name its leads (for example "Shift lead" for Front desk), and an area with no name set says "Area lead". Only the label changes: the role is still area lead.

## 7. Scheduling model

- **Weekly slots generate shifts** a set number of weeks ahead (setting, default 8). Each generated shift copies the slot's details and fills in its regulars, leaving out anyone whose account is turned off. All of a slot's regulars for that date go on, even when that is more than the shift's people needed.
- **Generation runs daily** (`ssvs:generate-shifts`; the other scheduled jobs are `ssvs:cover-requests`, `ssvs:change-heads-ups` and `ssvs:shift-reminders`, each every 10 minutes, sections 8.3, 8.4 and 10) and never changes a shift that already exists, so per-date edits survive. It skips dates covered by a closure and shifts that have already started. Adding a regular also adds them to the slot's already-generated shifts that haven't started, aren't cancelled and don't overlap another shift they're on.
- **Every week or every other week.** An every-other-week slot has an anchor date. On a weekly slot, each regular can also work every week or every other week, with their own anchor date, so two people can split one weekly slot by each holding alternate weeks. A slot's people needed is checked per date, so alternating regulars don't count against each other. People needed is the number a shift needs, not a limit (see the 2026-11-13 decision log row).
- **Overlapping shifts are allowed.** A day can have several shifts in the same area at overlapping times, each with its own people needed. Coordinators set the times; volunteers only take shifts that exist. One person can't be on two active shifts whose times overlap (back to back is fine): putting them on one is refused with the shift they're already on, and adding a regular leaves those dates out and says which.
- **People needed varies by slot.** Seasonal changes are made by editing the slot, which updates its future shifts nobody changed on their own (section 8.4). Editing a slot from a date forward, so earlier dates keep the old details, is "This shift and the ones after it" (section 8.4).
- **Times are stored in the organization's local time** for slots, so a weekly shift doesn't move by an hour when daylight saving time changes. Dated shifts store their start and end in UTC, plus the local date.
- **Exceptions are per date.** Releasing one date never changes the slot or the regular's other dates. A coordinator or area lead can remove someone from one shift, or from every shift they're on in a range of dates in the areas they manage; the assignment is deleted (the change log keeps who was on it), the person is emailed once, and the shift shows its open place in Needs attention. Ending a regular's time on a slot (section 8.6) removes them from the slot's shifts after the last date.
- **Closures** cancel the shifts they cover and notify everyone on them.
- **Shifts are never deleted.** Cancelling a shift sets a cancelled time and a reason, and the shift stays on the calendar marked as cancelled, so generation doesn't recreate it and a coordinator can see what happened. A shift cancelled by hand can be brought back with "Bring this shift back" (section 8.4). Assignments can be deleted, because the change log keeps who was on each shift.
- **Creating or editing a weekly slot generates its shifts right away**, for that slot only, using the same rules as the daily run.
- **Editing a weekly slot reaches the future shifts people are on** when nothing clashes. A staffed shift gets the slot's new details unless that would leave fewer shadow spots than shadows on it or double-book someone on it; then it keeps its old details and the form lists it with the reason. Each person who can sign in gets one email: when a shift's time changed it's the change notice that asks whether the new time works (section 8.4, item 6), otherwise a plain email listing their shifts whose title changed. Changing only people needed or shadow spots emails nobody. A shift whose new start for its date has already passed (today's shift, moved to an earlier time) keeps its own times, takes the other new details and is listed with the reason "The new time has already passed", so nobody is asked about a time that never applied. Shifts that have started are never changed.
- **Rotation slots** are weekly slots with no regular, filled one date at a time. They use the same model; the only difference is a sign-up screen that lists upcoming dates and lets a volunteer take several at once.

## 8. Core flows

### 8.1 Taking an open shift

Taking a shift needs a login. The "Take this shift" button in a cover request email is a normal link to the take page, so someone who isn't signed in logs in first and is taken straight back to it (section 13).

1. The volunteer opens the shift from Home, "All my shifts", or the link in a cover request email. Home lists the next 14 days: "Your next shifts" and "Open shifts you can take" (cover requests first, tagged "Needs cover", then by date; slots with no regular are tagged "No regular"). "Your next shifts" and "All my shifts" keep the person's cancelled shifts, tagged "Cancelled" with the reason and no "Can't make it" button.
2. For a cover request the take page asks "Can you cover this one?" with a "Just this date" button. For an open place in a weekly slot with no current or future regular, it asks "How long do you want it?": "Just this date", "Every [weekday]" or "Every other [weekday]". "Every other [weekday]" is offered only on a slot that repeats every week, and neither is offered on a rotation. Taking "every week" makes the person the regular from that date, which puts them on each date that has room and doesn't overlap another of their shifts, and fills the cover request on each of those dates. The email to them says which dates were skipped. It uses the same take page for any other open place, such as a one-time shift or a rotation date.
3. Only people cleared for regular shifts (section 9) can take a shift. A volunteer who isn't cleared sees "You'll be able to take shifts once a coordinator clears you." with no button, and this is checked again under the shift lock, so a take that lands after a coordinator moves them back is refused the same way. Skills aren't built yet; when they are, a shift that requires a skill the volunteer doesn't have will say so and not offer the button. Both checks live in `CoverEligibility`.
4. A person can't take a shift they're already on (as a regular or a shadow), one that has started or been cancelled, or one that overlaps another active shift they're on. The page says why in plain words.
5. **The first person to take it gets it.** The oldest open cover request on the shift is filled, so when two people released the same shift, each releaser hears who covered for them. Someone who loses the race, or follows an old link, sees "Someone already took this one" with other open shifts they could take. Pressing take twice lands on "It's yours".
6. On success, the volunteer gets "It's yours" and an email. The releaser gets "{Name} is covering your shift". Shadows get "back on" when their spot had been on hold (section 8.2). The area's active leads get "Covered" for a cover request, or "{Name} took" for any other open place. The organizer of an event shift is told too once events exist. Calendars update.
7. The person who released a shift doesn't get the cover request for it, but can take it back from Home while it's still open. A coordinator or area lead who puts someone on a released shift fills the cover request the same way: the releaser and shadows are told, but the leads aren't, since a lead or coordinator did it. So does every other change that puts a regular on a shift: a coordinator adding a regular to a weekly slot, bringing a cancelled shift back, or a closure or weekly slot change bringing a date back with its regulars. Each regular put on a shift fills its oldest open cover request, and each releaser gets one email listing their dates.
8. **Who gets the cover request email.** Everyone who can sign in, is cleared for regular shifts, has "Email me when someone can't make a shift I could take" turned on (the default, changed on the Emails page and logged as `user.email_settings_changed`), isn't on the shift, isn't on an overlapping active shift and didn't release it. The email names the releaser by first name and last initial, shows the note if there is one, and keeps the area's name as it's stored ("Can you cover Front desk on Thursday, October 22?"). The link at the bottom of each one goes to the Emails page, which needs a login. This one check is `CoverEligibility`; skills will join it when they're built.

### 8.2 "I can't make it"

1. The volunteer taps "Can't make it" on one of their shifts and chooses, with nothing picked to start with: just this date, several dates (a from and to date), or give up the weekly slot from a date forward (offered only on a shift they're the weekly regular for).
2. They can add an optional note for whoever covers, up to 500 characters. It can't include a web address (`://` or `www.`, in any capitals, even with invisible characters in between): the form says "This can't include a web address." and nothing is sent. It's trimmed, has invisible formatting characters removed, is shown as plain text to people taking the shift and in the emails, and stays on the cover request if the releaser's account is later removed, the same as a closure's reason.
3. **Several dates and the weekly slot get a check step.** It lists every shift they'd be off, and nothing is released until they confirm. A range that goes past the shifts on the calendar says so. For the weekly slot, an unreadable date is refused with "Pick a date." and a past date with "Pick a date from today on.".
4. **They're released as soon as they confirm**, and "You're off" says so. Responsibility moves to the cover process: each regular place they leave becomes one cover request, so a shift two people released has two (section 8.3). A shift that had more people than it needs still has them all when one of them comes off it, so no place opens: there's no cover request, nobody is asked, and their email and "You're off" say "Front desk still has the people it needs, so nobody else was asked." The release form and the check step say it before anything has happened, in the future tense: the form's version starts "If you're off just Thursday, October 22, Front desk still has the people it needs, so nobody else will be asked.", and the check step ends "so nobody else will be asked." Releasing one date or a range never changes their weekly regular.
5. **Giving up the weekly slot** ends their regular the day before the chosen date and releases every future, uncancelled shift of the slot they're on from then, each with a cover request. A shift that has already started is left alone, and they also come off cancelled future shifts of the slot without an email, the same as ending a regular. Volunteers get one email listing those dates, and the take page then offers "Every Thursday", because the slot has no regular. The usual "regular ended" email is replaced by the release email. With no shifts on the calendar from that date, the regular still ends and they get a short email saying they're no longer the regular.
6. **A shadow's spot is on hold only when nobody but shadows is left on the shift.** Nothing is stored for "on hold"; it's worked out from the assignments. Shadows get "on hold" at release, "back on" when a cover request is filled and the spot had been on hold, and "still has nobody to shadow" at 24 hours (section 8.3). They don't get "on hold" as well when the shift is released inside 24 hours.
7. **A shadow who can't make it** leaves the shadow spot. There's no cover request, and everyone with the coordinator role gets "{Name} can't make their shadow shift" with the shift's area and time and the shadow's note, if there is one, so they know the trainee won't be there. It's logged.
8. **Emails.** The person releasing gets one "You're off" email listing every place they left, shadow spots included. A release of only a shadow spot sends them no email, and the screen confirms it. One action sends each person at most one email of each kind, and one email failing doesn't stop the others. A release that loses a race, because the person was taken off the shift or it was cancelled in the meantime, lands on Home with "You're not on this shift any more.".

### 8.3 Cover requests and escalation

`ssvs:cover-requests` runs the ladder every 10 minutes, so the scheduler has to be running (DEVELOPMENT.md, and the install guide, docs/install.md). The timings are constants for now, 72 and 24 hours (`CoverLadder::RESEND_HOURS` and `URGENT_HOURS`). Per-area timings aren't built yet (section 14).

1. **At release:** everyone who could take the shift gets a cover request email (section 8.1) with a "Take this shift" link, naming the releaser by first name and last initial and showing their note. Posting to the area's chat channel comes with the chat plan. A person who releases several shifts at once sends one email listing them. A shift that still has the people it needs after the release gets no cover request at all (section 8.2), so it's neither emailed nor escalated.
2. **72 hours before, still open:** everyone who could take it now gets "Still open", once per shift. Only requests made before the 72 hour mark count toward it, so a shift released inside that window never gets one, since its first email went out inside it.
3. **24 hours before, still open:** the area's active leads and everyone with the coordinator role (not admins who aren't also coordinators) get the urgent email, each once. When nobody but shadows is left on the shift, its shadows get "still has nobody to shadow, please reach out to the volunteer coordinators to confirm next steps". Each request is escalated once, and cancelled shifts are skipped until they're brought back. Marking the shift as urgent on the public calendar comes with the public calendar.
4. **Released with less than 24 hours to go:** volunteers still get the cover request, since they're the ones who can fill it, and leads and coordinators get the urgent email at the same moment. A lead or coordinator who could cover it gets only the urgent email, not the cover request as well, and a lead or coordinator who released it gets neither. Shadows get only "still has nobody to shadow", not "on hold" as well. A range with several shifts inside 24 hours sends each lead, coordinator and shadow one merged email.
5. The first person to take it gets it (section 8.1). Later clicks see "Someone already took this one" and a list of other open shifts. Taking it stops the ladder for that request.
6. **Only requests for open places count.** The ladder and Home's "Needs cover" names count only a shift's oldest open requests, as many as it has open places, so a request left open by anything that put someone on the shift without filling it is never sent again or escalated.

### 8.4 Editing a shift

Editing a shift is built, including the change notices that ask people whether a new time works. This section describes what's built.

A coordinator or area lead edits a shift that belongs to a weekly slot, and the app asks "This shift" or "This shift and the ones after it".

1. "This shift" changes that one date, and later edits to the slot don't overwrite it. It's allowed when people are on it, except that it's refused when it would leave fewer shadow spots than shadows on it, or move the time so it overlaps another shift one of them is on. Lowering people needed below the people on it is allowed, since going over is allowed.
2. "This shift and the ones after it" opens the weekly slot form with a start date, the shift's date. It changes the slot and the shifts on or after that date that nobody changed on their own, including shifts people are on when nothing clashes (section 7). No slot is split: the regulars stay on, and the slot's own details change for good. Earlier dates keep their details: one that now differs from the slot is marked as changed on its own (and logged), so a later edit of the whole slot leaves it alone. A cancelled shift before the start date that is brought back later gets the slot's new details. The slot's day and how often it repeats still can't change while it has regulars or people on future shifts. The sidebar's and slot panel's "Edit the slot" open the form with no start date, which changes every future shift nobody changed on their own.
3. **Change emails.** A change to a shift's title alone emails everyone on it who can sign in, once, listing what changed, and a change to people needed or shadow spots alone emails nobody. A change to a shift's time is different: everyone on it who can sign in is asked whether the new time works (item 6) instead of getting a plain email, and a title change made in the same edit rides along as an extra line. A slot change sends each person one email listing all their changed shifts. The panel says so before saving: "The 1 person on it is emailed if the time or what it's for changes." (or "The {n} people on it are emailed if the time or what it's for changes."), and the choice step adds "If the time changes, everyone on the changed shifts is asked whether the new time works."
4. **Bring this shift back.** A shift cancelled by hand has a "Bring this shift back" button. A confirm step says how many people will be emailed that it's back on and who will be removed because they're now on another shift at that time. It's not offered, with the reason shown instead, when a closure cancelled the shift ("Cancelled by the {reason} closure. Change the closure to bring it back."), when the weekly slot no longer runs on that date ("The weekly slot doesn't run on this date any more. Change the slot to bring it back."), or when a later closure covers the date ("{Area} is closed that day: {reason}."). Shifts that have started are never brought back. A shift from a weekly slot that nobody changed on its own comes back with the slot's current details for that date (time, people needed and title, and shadow spots unless the slot's are fewer than the shadows on it), since slot changes skip cancelled shifts, and the slot's regulars who work that date and aren't turned off are put on it, including ones added while it was cancelled. A regular who was taken off that date before it was cancelled stays off it. A shift changed on its own comes back with its own details and only the people already on it. The "back on" email shows the shift's time as it comes back, and the confirm step counts the regulars it adds. When the slot's time changed while the shift was cancelled, the people who were on it get a change notice (item 6) in the same save, and their one email is the time email in place of "back on": it leads with "Your {Area} shift on {date} is back on, at a new time." and has both buttons. Regulars who join as it comes back never knew the old time, so they get "back on", and so does everyone whose shift came back at the same time it had.
5. **Remove from shift.** Each person on a shift has a "Remove from shift" link. The page asks "Which shifts?": just that date, or all of the person's shifts from one date to another, with a "Check which shifts" step that lists them before anything is removed. The range only includes shifts in areas the person using it manages, and says how many it left out. The range refuses with "Pick two dates." (a date missing or not a date), "Pick dates from today on." and "The last date can't be before the first date." When the range goes past the shifts on the calendar (the generation window), the check step adds "Shifts after {Weekday, Month day} aren't on the calendar yet, so they aren't included. To stop {Name} working those, end their time as a regular." The person is emailed once, listing every shift, and after it the status says "{Name} was removed from 1 shift." or "{Name} was removed from {n} shifts." and adds "They were emailed." only when at least one of the shifts wasn't cancelled and the person can sign in. Shifts that have started are never touched. A shift they're removed from shows its open place in Needs attention.

6. **Change notices: asking whether the new time works.** When a shift's time changes, each person on it who can sign in (regulars and shadows alike) gets a change notice and one email, "Your {Area} shift moved to {time}" (or "Your shifts changed"), with the old and new times and two buttons: "Still works for me" and "I can't make the new time". One person's email covers every shift that edit changed for them, so a weekly slot change that moves eight Thursdays asks once, and one answer covers all of them. Both buttons are normal links that need a login: someone who isn't signed in logs in first and comes straight back, the way "Take this shift" works. They aren't action links (section 10). A change to only the title or people needed doesn't ask. "I can't make the new time" in the email links to the answer page, which offers "Can't make it" for the earliest shift that hasn't started, so the link still works after the first shift has passed. The email adds "If you don't answer, your shift lead will check with you before the shift." only when the earliest shift starts more than 48 hours away, since no heads-up goes out inside that window. When a newer time change replaces a person's open notice, the new notice keeps the old one's "was" time, the last time they were asked about, not an unanswered time in between. A shift that comes back at a new time (item 4) is asked the same way. A change that puts a shift back at the time the person was last asked about (undone before they answered) asks nothing: the open notice ends, and the person gets no new notice and no email for that shift, though their other changed shifts in the same edit still go in their one email, and a title change made with the undo is still told (as a line in that email, or as the plain title email when it's their only change).
7. **The answer page.** "Still works for me" is a button on the page the email links to ("Your {Weekday} {Area} shift changed", with the new time, the old time and who changed it). It confirms the notice, says "Thanks. You're still on at the new time.", and logs `change_notice.confirmed`. After confirming, the page still offers "I can't make it after all". "I can't make the new time" opens "Can't make it" for those shifts (section 8.2), so the usual cover requests go out; it isn't stored as an answer, because leaving the shift through "Can't make it" ends the notice and is already logged as a release. An answer belongs to one change: a newer time change on the same shift replaces the person's open notice for it, and the older email's page says "This shift has changed again since that email." The page also says what happened when it can't be answered: confirmed already, the person is off the shift ("You're not on this shift any more."), the shift was cancelled ("This shift was cancelled.", also when some of the batch is cancelled and none is still ahead), the shift has started, or the person left the shift and was put back on it ("This shift has changed since that email.", "You're on this shift at its current time. This email's question doesn't apply any more."). Someone else's notice answers exactly like one that doesn't exist (a 404), and nothing is confirmed, released or noticed on a shift that has started or is cancelled.
8. **People who never open the email are still asked.** Home shows a card with both buttons for any time change the person hasn't answered, the shift panel on the Schedule tab asks the same question (one answer covers the whole batch, so when it covers more shifts the panel says which: "The time changed for your Thursdays from October 22 to December 10." for a run of one slot's dates, or "The time changed for 3 of your shifts.", and Home's card says how many shifts), and the person's shift card carries a "Time changed" tag. The card and the tag weren't wireframed and need LMN's check. On the coordinator's panel each person on the shift shows "Confirmed the new time" or "Hasn't answered yet", and the panel says when the time changed and what it was.
9. **The 48-hour heads-up.** `ssvs:change-heads-ups` runs every 10 minutes. When a shift starts in less than 48 hours and someone still on it hasn't answered its time change, the area's active leads who can sign in get one email per shift naming them (short names), or every active coordinator who can sign in when the area has no such lead. It goes once (`lead_told_at`), only for changes made more than 48 hours before the shift (a change made inside the 48 hours sends nothing, the same idea as "Still open" never going out for a shift released inside 72 hours), and is logged as `change_notice.lead_told` by the system. Nobody else is emailed. The scheduler has to be running for it (DEVELOPMENT.md).

### 8.5 Closures

A coordinator or admin adds a closure for one or more areas and a date range, with a reason. Affected shifts are cancelled and everyone on them is notified. Closures don't create cover requests.

- The form lists what it will cancel, with who is on each shift, before saving.
- Shifts that have started are never touched, and shifts already cancelled (by hand or by an earlier closure) stay as they are, so nobody is emailed twice for the same shift. Closures for the same area may overlap; both show.
- Each active person on the cancelled shifts gets one email listing all of their cancelled shifts. A later closure that overlaps an earlier one emails people only about the shifts it newly cancels. Cancelled shifts stay on the calendar, marked with the reason, and the nightly run never makes shifts for closed days, even when a closure is saved while it is running.
- A closure whose first day is before today is stored starting today, and one whose last day is before today is refused.
- A one-time shift can't be added on a day a closure covers in its area; the form says the area is closed and why.
- Closures show on the week and day views as an all-day bar at the top of each closed day, and on the month view as "Closed: {reason}". A slot edit never brings back a shift a closure cancelled.
- **Changing a closure.** Coordinators and admins (not area leads) open a closure from its label on the calendar. A closure for several areas is one closure per area, each changed on its own: a label shared by several areas reads "Closed: Thanksgiving. Change: Front desk, Shop", with a link for each area. The change page shows "What this changes": the shifts it would cancel and the shifts it would bring back, with who is on each. Days it no longer covers get their shifts back, newly covered days have theirs cancelled, and a new reason is copied onto the cancelled shifts without another email. A day that comes out from under a closure but whose weekly slot no longer runs on it stays cancelled with the reason "The weekly slot changed", and nobody is emailed. A shift that comes back gets its weekly slot's current details and regulars the same way "Bring this shift back" does (section 8.4), and the previews count those regulars among the people emailed. Anyone on a shift that comes back who is now on another shift at that time is removed from it. Each person gets one email per change, covering shifts cancelled and shifts brought back together. A shift that comes back at a new time because its slot moved while it was closed gets change notices (section 8.4, item 6), and the person's one email is then the time email, which carries their other shifts that are back on and shifts cancelled as lines after the question. Shifts missing for days that reopened are made right after the change is saved.
- **Limits on changing.** A closure that has started keeps its first day, and its last day can't move before yesterday ("The last day can't be before yesterday."); ending it yesterday reopens today. A closure that has ended can't be changed ("This closure has ended. To close more days, add a new closure."), and one that has started says "This closure has already started, so only its last day can change. To fix days that have passed, remove it."
- **Removing a closure**, including one whose days have passed. Future shifts it cancelled come back (or pass to another closure that covers the day, or stay cancelled when the slot no longer runs that day), and people on them are emailed ("back on", or the time email when a shift comes back at a new time, section 8.4, item 4). Days that have passed lose the "Closed" label and their shifts stay cancelled. The screen says so: "Days that have passed lose the Closed label. Their shifts stay cancelled."

### 8.6 Volunteer hours and credit

SSVS is the record of volunteer hours.

- **Ending a regular.** A coordinator or area lead ends a regular's time on a weekly slot with "End regular" on the shift panel, choosing the last date they work. They're removed from every shift of that slot after that date, and emailed about the future ones. The date can be in the past, so the record matches when they really stopped. In that case they're also removed from the past shifts after that date, so those hours don't count as worked, but only after a confirmation that lists the past shifts ("{Name} is on {n} past shifts after {date}. Ending their time as a regular removes them from those too, so they don't count as worked:"). Nobody is emailed about past shifts. Only the regular's own spots within that stint are removed: a later stint as a regular on the same slot is left alone, and shadow spots stay. A later last date than the one a regular already has is refused ("To extend it, add them as a regular again."). A regular ended before they ever started is stored with an end the day before their start and counts as never current, so it doesn't stop the person being added as a regular again for overlapping dates. In that case the email and the status say they're no longer the regular without naming the date, and the status leaves out the shifts sentence when they weren't removed from any.

- **A shift counts as worked once it ends**, unless its lead marks a no-show. Leads can correct a mark afterwards. Every mark and correction is in the change log.
- **Hours** are the length of each shift a person worked. Each person sees their own hours; leads see hours worked in their areas, and coordinators and admins see everyone's, filtered by person, area and date range.
- **Credit requests.** An organization can let volunteers turn hours into credit, such as credit toward a membership. A volunteer asks for credit for hours they haven't claimed yet, and a small group of people reviews the queue and approves or declines each request. Hours in an approved request can't be claimed again. Hours and credit requests come in phase 4. Who reviews, and what an hour is worth, are open questions (section 19).

## 9. New volunteers, shadowing and clearance

- New volunteers are invited like everyone else, but **start not cleared**. The flag is worded positively ("Cleared for regular shifts") so that forgetting to change it is the safe mistake. Only cleared people take shifts and get cover requests (section 8.1). Coordinators and area leads can still put anyone on a shift from the calendar, and the person picker tags someone who isn't cleared "Not cleared yet". Everyone who can sign in sees the full schedule on the Schedule tab, read-only for volunteers (sections 6 and 13), including someone who isn't cleared yet; their shift panel offers "Shadow this shift" where a shadow spot is open, and "Take this shift" never appears for them.
- **Holding a role clears you.** Granting any role clears someone who isn't cleared yet, logged as `user.cleared` with `via` `role.granted`. Taking the role away leaves them cleared, so a former lead stays cleared. When clearance was added, everyone who already held a role was cleared by the system (`via` `migration`).
- **Shadow spots** are added to existing shifts by a lead or coordinator. Not-cleared volunteers see their progress ("Shadow shifts done: 1 of 2"), their next shadow shift and the open shadow spots for the next 14 days on Home, each with a "Shadow this shift" button that opens a page with one button to confirm. A shadow spot is offered only on a shift with at least one regular on it, so a trainee always has someone to learn from: a shift with shadow spots but nobody in a regular place (a slot with no regular, or a released shift) isn't listed and refuses sign-up with "Nobody is on this shift to shadow yet." The card says who they'd shadow ("With Jamie"). Two people pressing it for the last spot get it once; the other sees "There's no shadow spot left on this shift." Signing up is logged as `assignment.added` with `via` `shadow_signup`, the person gets "You're shadowing {Area} on {date}", and the area's active leads get "{Name} is shadowing {Area}, {date}".
- **Cleared people don't take shadow spots.** They see "Shadow spots are for people who aren't cleared yet. You can take shifts instead."
- **A shadow shift counts once it ends**, unless it was cancelled or a lead (in their area) or a coordinator marks the shadow a no-show. Marking is offered on the shift panel only once the shift has ended, only for shadow places (no-shows for regular places come with volunteer hours, phase 4), can be undone, and is logged as `assignment.no_show`. A shadow place someone left never counts, and neither does a shift still in progress.
- **Required shadow shifts is a coordinator setting** ("Shadow shifts before review", default 2, from 1 to 10, in the Shadowing section of Settings, which coordinators can reach; the rest of Settings stays admin-only). Changing it never un-clears anyone; it only changes when the "Ready for review" prompt appears. Lowering it shows people who now meet it as Ready for review right away.
- When a volunteer reaches the required number, coordinators and admins see **"Ready for review: {Name}"** in the calendar's Needs attention list, whatever areas are shown, linking to the person page. The person page's "Shadowing and clearance" section shows their progress and shadow history (each ended shadow shift, with who they shadowed and whether it was done, a no-show or cancelled) to everyone who can see the page. Clearing is always a coordinator decision; the app never clears anyone automatically, and marking a no-show takes someone off Ready for review.
- **Coordinators can override in both directions:** clear someone early with "Clear for regular shifts", or move a cleared volunteer back to shadowing. Each takes an optional note (up to 500 characters) that goes only in the change log, never in an email. Clearing is logged as `user.cleared` with `via` `person_page` and emails the person "You're cleared for regular shifts". Someone turned off can't be cleared until they're turned back on.
- **Clearing keeps someone's upcoming shadow places.** They can keep shadowing or release them with "Can't make it".
- **Moving someone back** is refused for anyone who holds a role ("Remove their role first."). It's a page of its own that asks what to do with that person's upcoming regular shifts, when they have any or are a weekly regular: keep them, or release them. Releasing ends their regulars yesterday, so they're no longer the regular on any date from today and the nightly generator doesn't put them back, and releases each future regular place through the normal cover flow: each one becomes a cover request naming them, and one inside 24 hours escalates at once, with the coordinator who moved them back left out of the urgent email. That coordinator isn't sent the cover request either, since they made the gap themselves; a later "Still open" resend still goes to everyone who could take the shift. A shift that still has the people it needs once they're off it gets no cover request. Releasing also takes them off their cancelled future shifts quietly, with no cover request. Those releases are logged as the coordinator's. The person gets one "You've been moved back to shadowing" email listing what was released, split into the shifts other volunteers were asked to cover and the ones that still have the people they need, instead of "You're off". It's logged as `user.moved_back`, with the keep or release choice. Their shadow places stay.
- **Moving back starts the shadow count over.** Only shadow shifts that end after the move back (`users.shadowing_since`) count toward review again, while the shadow history still lists every one, with the ones that ended before the move back marked "Done (before moving back)".
- **The import never undoes a move back.** Running `ssvs:import-people --cleared-tag` again leaves alone anyone a coordinator moved back to shadowing, listed in the preview under "Moved back to shadowing, left alone", and anyone turned off.
- **A shadow who becomes the regular** has their shadow place turned into a regular place on the same assignment, freeing the shadow spot, logged as `assignment.type_changed`. That happens on the slot's shifts from the start date, ones that haven't started and aren't cancelled, when a coordinator or lead makes them the regular from the weekly "Add a regular" screen, or for one date from the shift panel, which lists people shadowing the shift in its person search tagged "Shadowing".
- Clearing and moving back are under the change log's Clearance kind; no-shows and a shadow made the regular are under Schedule.
- Skills (granted by leads, required by some shifts) are planned later.

## 10. Notifications

In the email and status copy in this spec, "{Name}" is a person's first name and last initial ("Jordan P.", or just "Cher" for a one-word name), and a removed person is "Removed person" on coordinator screens and in the change log. A removed person who released a shift is "Someone" on the take page, on Home and in the emails to volunteers and leads (no release screen names a releaser). The only copy that uses a first name alone is a greeting to the person themselves ("Hi Sam" on Home and at the top of each email).

- **Channels:** email (always available), chat webhooks, and text messages. Each person picks which channels get which kinds of notice: cover requests, changes to my shifts, reminders, and (for leads and coordinators) escalations.
- **Email is the baseline.** Chat only reaches people who are active there, so it can't be the only channel for anything that matters.
- **Chat** goes through a generic outgoing-webhook layer, with Discord as the first adapter. Matrix, Zulip, Slack, and Mattermost all accept incoming webhooks, so switching chat platforms is a configuration change.
- **Text messages** come in phase 3. Email and chat are the channels until then, and notifications are written so a text channel can be added later without changing them. US senders need carrier registration (10DLC or toll-free verification) before texting, which can take weeks. Phone numbers need a confirmation code and an explicit opt-in per notice type. Carrier email-to-text gateways are not an option; the major US carriers have shut them down or are shutting them down.
- **Action links:** links in emails and texts are signed for that recipient, single-use, and short-lived (setting, default 7 days or until the shift starts, whichever comes first). Each link does exactly one thing, such as confirming one change, and never opens a full session. Action links aren't built yet. The two buttons in the change email ("Still works for me" and "I can't make the new time") are normal links that need a login, the same as the take link below, so they aren't action links. Claiming a shift isn't an action link either: the "Take this shift" button in a cover request email is a normal link to the take page, and taking a shift needs a login (section 8.1). A forwarded email therefore can't be used to get into someone's account. Links posted in chat are public, so they require a normal login first, and so does the take link in a cover request email.
- **Cover request emails:** each volunteer can turn off "Email me when someone can't make a shift I could take" (on by default) on the Emails page. It only affects the cover request and "Still open" emails; emails about their own shifts, and the urgent emails leads and coordinators get, aren't turned off by it. Every cover request email links to that page, which needs a login. The same page has a second panel, "Reminders", with "Remind me before my shifts" (on by default), which turns off the one reminder email before each shift the person is on. Both checkboxes save together with one Save button, and each change is logged as `user.email_settings_changed` with the old and new value of every setting that changed.
- **Reminders (built):** `ssvs:shift-reminders` runs every 10 minutes and emails each person on a shift once, the number of hours before it starts that admins chose in Settings (off, or 2, 12, 24 or 48; default 24; section 14). Everyone on the shift gets one: regulars, people who took it, and shadows. People who can't sign in (not invited yet, turned off, or the shared "Removed person" record) get nothing, and neither do people who turned off "Remind me before my shifts" on the Emails page. Someone put on the shift after the reminder time (inside the window) gets none, because they just signed up. Cancelled shifts and shifts that have started get none. Each person on a shift is reminded once (`assignments.reminded_at`, set whether or not an email goes out), so a later time change doesn't send a second reminder; the change email covers it. The email says today, tomorrow or the date (worked out from the organization's date, in the organization's time zone, which the body names, such as "Eastern Time"), lists who else is on the shift and who is shadowing, has a "Can't make it" button (left out when the person hasn't answered a time change notice for that shift, because the email then has "Still works for me" and "I can't make the new time" instead, and left out for a shadow on a shift with no regulars, who is told nobody is there to shadow and asked to reach out to the volunteer coordinators to confirm next steps, matching the cover ladder's "still nobody to shadow" email), and ends with a link to the Emails page. One `shift.reminded` entry is logged as the system for each shift that emailed anyone (shift id and count, no names). Per-channel choices wait for chat.
- **Email wording (built):** the default wording of all 32 emails (subjects, greetings, lines and button labels) is in `lang/en/emails.php`, one array per email with one line each. A line holds `{variable_name}` placeholders (snake_case in braces, such as `{first_name}`) that the code fills in, and a comment above each line says when it shows and what each variable holds. A line that reads differently for one or several has an `one` and an `other` key. Lists and joins, such as "Alex R., Jordan P. and Sam E.", are built in code and passed in as one variable, and text people typed (a release note, a shift title) is passed in the same way. Every value filled into a line (people's names, area names, titles, reasons, the organization's name) reads exactly as typed, in every line, default or edited, in the HTML and the text part: HTML special characters and Markdown punctuation in a value become entity codes, so a value can never become a link, an image or formatting, and a value at the start of a line can't turn it into a list or heading. The same goes for the organization's name in the header, under "Regards," and in the footer. Links and the bold date are built by the code (`EmailText::link()` and `EmailText::bold()`) and go in as they are. Subjects are plain text and are left as typed. A missing key or a placeholder left unfilled throws in every environment, so it shows up in the tests and never in an inbox, and a test renders every email and fails on any leftover placeholder. Wording that includes a fixed duration (10, 15 or 60 minutes, 7 days, 24, 48 and 72 hours) states the real limit in plain text, so changing those words doesn't change the limit. The name in the header, under "Regards," and in the footer, and the sender's name, are the organization's name from Settings, and the footer year is the current year; neither is wording that can be edited.
- **The email editor (built, step 2):** active coordinators and admins open "Emails we send" (`/emails`) from the calendar toolbar, next to People and Change log, or from Home. The page lists every email grouped by who gets it. Picking one shows each of its lines in its own box with a hint saying when the line shows, the words in braces it can use (each with an example, such as {first_name} for "Sam"), and a preview of the email using example people ("Sam", "Front desk", "Thursday, October 22") and what's typed in the boxes, without saving or sending anything. A "Show it as" choice picks the version to preview (the reminder, for example, has one for someone on the shift, a shadow, a shadow with nobody to shadow, and someone with an unanswered time change). On a phone the preview shows above the boxes after pressing Preview, and on a computer it sits beside them; pressing Enter in a box previews rather than saves. Save writes only the lines that were changed. "Back to the default" on a line deletes the edit, so a later improvement to the default reaches it, and keeps whatever else was typed in other boxes. Text that matches the default is saved as no edit. Until an organization edits a line, every email reads as the file says.
  - **Edited text is plain text.** Whatever is typed reaches the email exactly as typed, in the HTML and the text part: Markdown, HTML and entity codes show as the characters typed and never become formatting, markup or a link. The links and bold dates the default lines used to build with Markdown (the shift links, "Stop getting cover requests", the reminders' "Emails page" link, the leads' bold date) come in through variables built by the code, such as `{shift_link}` and `{emails_page_link}`, so an edit can move or drop them but never change where a link points.
  - **What a save refuses**, with a plain message and nothing stored or logged: a word in braces the default line doesn't have (a misspelling gets a "Did you mean" suggestion); a link variable (any ending in `_link`) or `{note}` the default line has, taken out, because without it people lose the only way to stop cover requests or reach the Emails page, or a volunteer's own note; a web address (any text with `://` or `www.`); a cleared or all-space box ("Type the wording, or use Back to the default.", so a slip of the keyboard never resets a line); text over 1000 characters; and, in the footer, the characters Markdown reads (`[ ] * _` and backtick, `~ | \ < >`) and entity codes such as `&#58;`, which would otherwise decode into a live address. Unicode format characters (bidirectional overrides and zero-width characters) are removed, so the text shown is the text stored, and runs of whitespace become one space. A form that was open while someone else saved the same line is refused for that line ("Someone else changed this line while you were editing") instead of overwriting it.
  - **Every email.** A page named "Every email" holds the four lines Laravel adds around our own: "Hello!" when an email has no greeting, "Regards,", the "If you're having trouble clicking" line under each button (its `{button}` is the button's label) and "All rights reserved." in the footer. They're applied when an email is sent, through the translator's missing-key callback (`App\Notifications\LaravelMailLines`), so no Laravel mail view is published or copied. An edit to the "trouble clicking" line can't use a colon followed by `actionText` to mean the button, because the colon is escaped.
  - **A bad edit never stops an email.** If a stored edit somehow breaks a rule (written before a release removed a variable, or put in the database by hand), the email goes out with the default line and the problem is reported, so a cover request to 30 people is never stopped halfway. Each email built reports once, so one bad line can report many times.
  - **Not editable:** "to" in time ranges (`App\Calendar\TimeFormat`), plural weekdays ("Thursdays"), "every other" before a weekday (`TakeShift`), "Someone" for a removed person (`User::releaserName()` and `CoverRequest`), and the role names passed into emails ("a coordinator", "a shift lead for Front desk"). They're shared with the screens or are formatting more than wording. The header, sign-off name and footer name come from Settings.
  - **The change log** has an Emails kind with `email_wording.changed` and `email_wording.reset`. Each entry shows the text before and after (the default's text when the line wasn't edited), and has the email and line keys in details and no names. The change log has an email filter that shows one email's history, including the lines of other emails that the email's page lists; it works only with the Emails kind or no kind. A release overwrites `lang/en/emails.php` (the defaults) but never the edits, which are stored in the database (`email_wordings`), so an upgrade doesn't lose them.

## 11. Integrations

Each one is optional and off by default.

- **Member directory sync.** A scheduled job reads people from an external membership system and creates invitations or deactivates accounts to match. It matches people by their ID in the membership system, never by email address, because volunteers can change their own address. The first adapter is Hello Club, which has an API with key-based access and a rate limit of about 30 requests per minute (per an unofficial guide; confirm against the vendor's docs). Until that sync exists, an admin can load the volunteer list from a Hello Club members export with `ssvs:import-people` (section 13). It stores each person's membership ID (the export's Profile ID column) so the sync can recognize them later; confirm against Hello Club's API documentation that this is the ID its API uses before building the sync.
- **Hours export,** for organizations that keep hours somewhere else as well. When a shift ends, the app sends the completed assignment (person, date, area, duration, regular or shadow) to a webhook, so a separate system can record volunteer hours without anyone typing them twice.
- **Credit to the membership system (later goal).** Approved credit requests could be added to the member's record in the membership system automatically, starting with Hello Club. That needs write access, unlike member sync, so it would be the one exception to read-only access (section 13).
- **Google Calendar, one way.** The app publishes shifts to organization-owned Google calendars (one per area, or one combined) through a Google service account. No volunteer has to connect a Google account. Two-way sync is out of scope; it needs per-user Google sign-in, which brings Google's app verification review and a lot of breakage.
- **Personal calendar feeds.** Every volunteer gets a private iCal feed of their own shifts, with an "Add to my calendar" button on the home screen. The feed address is a secret, so it shows only that person's shifts (no other volunteers' names) and has a "reset my calendar link" button that invalidates the old address.

## 12. Public calendar

A public page lists events and areas marked public, with counts such as "needs 2 more volunteers". It never shows volunteer names. Members are sent to log in; everyone else is sent to the organization's "become a volunteer" page.

## 13. Security and privacy

Volunteers trust the organization with their contact details and their whereabouts. The app should hold as little as it needs, show it only to the people who need it, and be safe to run for organizations without a security team.

### Accounts and access

- **Invite-only.** Accounts exist only after a coordinator invites someone, directly or through member sync. People brought in by the volunteer import exist before they're invited, but they can't sign in or get any email until a coordinator invites them.
- **Login:** password, or a one-time email link. Passwords are hashed with a modern algorithm (bcrypt or Argon2id); these endpoints are rate-limited: the password login (5 a minute for each address and IP address, and 20 failed attempts an hour for each address from anywhere; a login that works isn't counted toward the hourly limit, and a blank address or one that isn't plain has no hourly limit, since the form refuses it anyway), the login link request (3 in 10 minutes for each address and IP address, and 10 a day for each address) and use (10 a minute per IP address), asking for a password reset link (5 a minute per IP address and 10 a day for each address, on top of Laravel's own wait of 60 seconds for each address), the app code, recovery code and emailed code routes (5 a minute, 20 an hour and 50 a day for each account, shared between them), asking for an emailed code (3 in 10 minutes for each account), the password reset form (10 a minute per IP address), confirming your password and changing your password (5 in 10 minutes for each person, each), changing your address (and its confirmation links) and the email editor. When the daily limit for an address stops a login link or reset link request, the answer is the same as always (status, page, message, headers and time) and no email is sent, so it never tells anyone whether someone else has asked about that address. A person whose address reaches a daily limit can still sign in with their password, and the limits reset the next day. One whose address reaches the hourly password limit can still sign in with an emailed link. The answers to these three sign-in requests carry no rate limit headers. A failed password login takes at least 750 milliseconds (`auth.timebox_duration`) whatever the address, so its timing doesn't reveal whether the address has a usable account. The person gets one email a day ("Someone is trying to sign in to your account") when ten wrong two-factor codes have been typed in a day or the hourly or daily limit stops codes, whichever comes first. Asking for a reset link and using one both take at least 750 milliseconds (`auth.timebox_duration`), so the answer takes the same time for every address. The password reset form answers the same way for an unknown address, a not-invited one and a real one: "This reset link has expired or was already used. Ask for a new one." Passwords need at least 10 characters. Adding a first password (the "Add a password" form on Login and security) needs an emailed-link sign-in by the same person in the last 15 minutes (a sign-in link, an invitation link or a confirm-it's-you link), the same proof of inbox control an email change asks of someone without a password. The form shows only during those 15 minutes. Otherwise the page says "To keep your account safe, we only add a password within 15 minutes of you signing in with an emailed link. Log out, ask for a new link on the login page, and come back here after you open it." with a "Log out to get a new link" button, and a form sent too late is refused with "To add a password, sign in again with an emailed link, then add it within 15 minutes." People who must set up two-factor are exempt, since setup is how they get a first password, and for them saving it counts as just confirming it so the authenticator app can start straight away. Whoever adds a first password gets an email, "A password was added to your account", with a link to reset it. Login-link requests and password reset requests answer the same way for every address, so they never reveal who has an account. Email addresses are plain printable ASCII letters, numbers and symbols, with no accents or invisible characters. Wherever one is typed (log in, login link, password reset, changing your address, inviting, the import and `ssvs:setup`) it's trimmed and lowercased first, and an address with anything else is refused with the same message for every address, so each account has exactly one spelling and the sign-in limits count it once. Spaces and invisible characters (such as a non-breaking space or a zero-width character) at the start or end are removed first, since pasting an address often brings them along. Inside the address they're refused. A deactivated account gets no login link or reset email, with the same answer on screen. Opening an emailed link shows a page with a button rather than logging in straight away, because some email security scanners open every link in a message. A login link keeps the page the person was heading to, such as a shift's take page, stored on the link itself so it still works when it opens in another browser or on another device, and after the two-factor code. Only a path inside SSVS is kept (up to 500 characters); anything else is dropped and they land on Home.
- **Login, invitations and two-factor setup are built on Laravel Fortify**, Laravel's first-party authentication backend, with our own screens.
- **Two-factor authentication is required** for admins, coordinators and area leads, since they can all see volunteers' contact details. They also need a password. The second factor is a code from an authenticator app, with one-time recovery codes. If the phone isn't available, a code can be emailed instead, but only after a password login: after an email-link login the inbox has already been used once, so an emailed code wouldn't be a second factor. For the same reason, after a password reset by email the emailed code is refused until the person next logs in with their authenticator app or a recovery code, or connects a new app. A reset also emails the person to say their password was changed. Someone who has lost both their phone and their recovery codes asks an admin, who can reset their two-factor after checking in person or by phone that it's really them, never only by email or chat. The reset logs them out everywhere, emails them to say it happened, and goes in the change log; they set up a new app and get new recovery codes the next time they log in with their password.
- **Action links in emails and texts do one thing only** (section 10) and never open a full session. Login, invitation and confirm-it's-you links are login links, not action links.
- **Sessions** expire after 120 minutes of inactivity, and a person can sign out of all devices from their profile. Deactivating a person ends their open sessions on their next request, and so does anyone who can't sign in, such as someone not invited yet. Changing your password ends your sessions on every other device and keeps the one you're using, and resetting a forgotten password ends them on every device. Either way remember-me cookies on other devices stop working. The device in use gets a new session id and, if it was remembered for them, a new remember-me cookie, so "Keep me logged in" keeps working there.
- **Changing your email address.** Anyone can change their own address after confirming who they are again: with their password (plus their app code, for area leads and up), or with a link emailed to their current address if they have no password (adding a first password doesn't count as confirming: a person who just added one confirms with it in the usual way). We then email a link to the new address, and the change happens only when that link is opened, so a typo can't lock anyone out; until then the old address keeps working. The old address gets an email saying the login address changed, showing the new one partly hidden, so the real owner hears about a change they didn't make. The change logs the person out on every other device, keeps the one they used signed in (and, if that device was remembered for them, gives it a new remember-me cookie, so "Keep me logged in" keeps working there), and goes in the change log. An address another account already uses gets the same answer on screen, so the form never reveals who has an account. Typing your own current address gets a plain "That's already your address.", since that says nothing about anyone else.
- **Not invited yet.** A person who has been added but not yet invited (`invited_at` is empty) gets no email of any kind and can't sign in by link, password or password reset. Every screen answers for them exactly as it does for an address with no account, so nothing reveals the difference. If a session for them exists anyway, it ends on its next request and they land on the login page with no message. Inviting them is what changes that. Accounts that existed before this status was added count as invited. Coordinators and admins invite one person from the person page or the invite form, or everyone not invited yet from the people list.
- **First-run setup:** `php artisan ssvs:setup` creates the organization and its first admin, and prints that admin's invitation link so an install works before email is configured.
- **Importing the volunteer list:** `php artisan ssvs:import-people {file} --as=<admin email>` reads a Hello Club members export, shows who it would add, who already exists and who it skipped (with the reason), and saves nothing until the admin says yes. It reads only the Full name, Email and Profile ID columns (and Categories, which it reads but uses only when `--lead-tag` or `--cleared-tag` is given) and never stores, logs or prints anything else. New people are added as not invited yet, so nobody is emailed. People who already exist are left alone, except that someone matched by email with no membership ID gets it filled in, logged as `user.member_id_added`. `--lead-tag` with `--lead-area` makes everyone with that category an area lead of that area. `--cleared-tag` clears everyone with that category for regular shifts (section 9), new people and people already imported, logged as `user.cleared` with `via` `import`; people who are already cleared are not logged again. Categories are matched exactly, the same way for both options.
- **Edited email wording can't add links or markup.** Coordinators and admins can change what every volunteer receives (`manage-emails`), so the editor is limited to active coordinators and admins, who all have two-factor, and every route (open, save, reset, preview) checks it. Typed text is escaped as plain text, so HTML, Markdown and entity codes show as typed, and a save refuses `://` and `www.` (look-alike punctuation counts: full-width and other compatibility characters are folded to plain ones first, the ideographic full stop counts as a full stop, and the blank Hangul filler characters are ignored, so `https：//` and `www．` are refused too) and refuses to take out a link variable or `{note}`. Saving, resetting and previewing are rate limited per person (30 a minute, shared). Every edit and every reset is in the change log with the old and new text. The remaining risk: a mail app may turn a bare domain such as example.org/path, or a `mailto:` or `tel:` address, into a link, and that isn't blocked, because catching it would mean guessing at web domains and refusing ordinary sentences. It would take a compromised coordinator or admin account to type one, and the change log would show who and what.
- **Values in emails read exactly as typed.** Every name, area name, title, reason and the organization's name filled into an email has its HTML and Markdown punctuation escaped in every line, default or edited, so even a value stored before the checks below existed, or one that bypassed them, can't become a link, an image or formatting (section 10).
- **Text people type that reaches an email can't include a web address, and names can't include link characters.** Release notes, shift and weekly slot titles, cancel and closure reasons, area names, lead names and the organization name refuse `://` and `www.` (any capitals, invisible formatting characters ignored) with "This can't include a web address." (look-alike punctuation such as full-width `：` and `．` counts as `://` and `www.`), using the same check as the email editor, so no account is held to a weaker rule than the editor's. A person's name (inviting, the Hello Club import, `ssvs:setup`) also refuses `[`, `]`, `<` and `>`, and is refused if it has control characters (other than tabs and line breaks), with "This name has characters we can't use.", and is stored tidied: tabs and line breaks turned into spaces and invisible characters removed (the zero-width joiner and non-joiner stay, since some languages need them), and runs of whitespace, non-breaking spaces included, made one space. The import skips a row with such a name and says why, and its screen output never shows control characters from the file. Bare domains such as example.org are still accepted, for the same reason as in the editor.
- **Change log** for every change to stored data, including clearance, roles, accounts and the schedule. Each entry says who made the change (a person, or the system for automatic jobs), what changed, with the old and new values where there are any (secrets show only that they changed), and when. Coordinators and admins can filter it by who made a change, who it was about, which record, which kind of change and a date range. Entries are never edited. Password hashes, tokens and two-factor secrets are never copied into it.
- **Pages send security headers.** Every answer, error pages and the maintenance mode page included (the server error and maintenance pages are standalone, since the database may be what's down), carries a Content-Security-Policy that lets scripts, stylesheets, fonts and images load only from the install itself, forbids framing the site (`frame-ancestors 'none'` and `X-Frame-Options: DENY`), and limits form posts and the base address to the site. It also sends `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Referrer-Policy: same-origin` and a Permissions-Policy that turns off the camera, microphone, location, payment and USB. In production over HTTPS it sends `Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000`, with no subdomains and no preload. The one loosening is style attributes (`style-src-attr 'unsafe-inline'`), because the calendar grid places each shift block with a computed top and height. Inline scripts and style elements are never allowed, and a test fails if a view has one (`App\Http\Middleware\SecurityHeaders`).
- **A production server can't run with the debug page on or log its emails.** When `APP_ENV=production`, the app turns `APP_DEBUG` off at start-up and writes a critical log line saying so every time the app starts (each request, `php artisan` run and scheduled command) until the setting is fixed, and any attempt to send email through the `log` mailer throws before anything is written, so login links and codes never reach a log file. The default mailer is `smtp`, and the failover mailer is just `smtp`, with no `log` in it.

### Who sees what

| Information | Volunteers | Leads (their areas) | Coordinators and admins | Public |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The schedule, with each person's first name and last initial | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Email addresses and phone numbers | Their own | Yes | Yes | No |
| Notification settings | Their own | No | Admins only | No |
| Clearance status and shadow history | Their own shadow progress count, on Home (not the history list) | Yes | Yes | No |
| Last login and last seen | Their own | Yes | Yes | No |
| Volunteer hours and credit requests | Their own | Hours in their areas | Yes | No |
| Open shifts and "needs N more" counts | Yes | Yes | Yes | Only areas and events marked public, never names |

The person picker tags anyone it lists who isn't cleared with "Not cleared yet", even a person the lead can't otherwise see, because a lead needs it to choose who to put on a shift. That is the one intended exception to clearance being shown only for people they can see.

Full names, email addresses and phone numbers stay with the roles that can see the person, so a volunteer never sees another person's full name, email address, phone number or person page link on the schedule. New volunteers who aren't cleared yet can see the full schedule. That includes when someone is working alone, so each deployment should decide whether that's acceptable.

Who an area lead can see comes from real working connections: a shift in their areas, or a regular slot there whose end date isn't before its start date (a regular ended before its start doesn't count; one ended after its start but before its first slot day still does). A regular can't be added with a first date before today in the organization's time zone, or with dates that include no day the slot runs ("Those dates don't include a day this slot runs. Pick dates that do."), so adding one backwards isn't a way to make someone visible. The person search matches an email address only for coordinators and admins, however many areas someone leads. For anyone else it matches any part of the name of a person the searcher can see, and matches anyone else only when the search is the start of their short name ("Robin E." matches "rob", "robin e" and "robin e.", ignoring capitals, accents and a final period, so "Ana C" finds Ana de la Cruz and "jose e" finds José Example), so it can't be used to work out a surname that isn't shown. Those checks run over the first 50 people whose name contains the search's first word (or, for coordinators and admins, whose email address contains the search), and the first 8 that pass are shown, people the searcher can see first in full-name order, then the rest by short name and then id. A search shorter than 2 characters once a trailing period is dropped ("..", "a.") is too short.

### Data we keep, and for how long

- **Stored:** name, email, membership ID (when the organization's membership system gives one), optional phone number, roles, skills, clearance status, notification settings, last login and last seen times, and schedule history. Nothing else is required.
- **Phone numbers** are only stored after the person confirms them with a code, and are deleted when they turn text messages off.
- **Inactive accounts** (not seen and no shifts for a set period; setting, default 18 months) are flagged to coordinators for removal.
- **Removal on request:** a coordinator or admin can delete a person's account and contact details. Their past shifts, regular slots and change log entries move onto one shared "Removed person" record (email `removed-person@invalid`, never able to sign in, never listed), and their own row is deleted, so nothing left can tell one removed person from another. It also blanks their name, email address and membership ID in the change log, rewrites their user ID wherever it is stored inside change log details and changes (assignment and regular entries, the people told about a cancelled shift, the regulars a slot change put back) to the shared record, and deletes their password reset tokens. The entries themselves stay. Removal first removes them from their future shifts and ends their regular slots, and is refused only if something lands on their schedule in between (someone puts them on a shift, say), in which case trying again releases that too. Only an admin can remove an admin, nobody can remove themselves, and it can't be undone. Login throttling can hold the address in the cache for up to a day after removal (the daily limits on login links and reset links for each address, the hourly limit on failed password logins for each address, and the shorter limits keyed by email and IP address). No account may use an address ending in `@invalid`, so nobody can take the shared record's address.
- **Export on request:** a person can download the information the app holds about them.

### Secrets and integrations

- **No secrets in the repository.** API keys (member sync, Google service account, SMS provider), SMTP passwords and chat webhook addresses live only in the server's environment configuration. The repository ships an example config with placeholders.
- **Chat webhook addresses are treated as secrets.** Anyone with one can post into that channel.
- **Integrations get the least access they need:** read-only member sync where the provider allows it, and a Google service account that can only write to the organization's scheduling calendars.
- **Outgoing messages never include phone numbers or email addresses** of other volunteers.
- **Search engines are asked not to index SSVS, and it serves no stored files.** robots.txt allows crawling so crawlers can read the `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow` header that every response carries (a page blocked in robots.txt can't carry the header and can still be listed as a bare address), and Laravel's file download and upload routes are turned off.

### Running the server

docs/install.md covers, for AlmaLinux:

- automatic security updates (dnf-automatic);
- SELinux left in enforcing mode, with the policy the app needs documented;
- firewalld allowing only HTTPS, HTTP (for the redirect to HTTPS and getting certificates) and SSH for admins;
- HTTPS everywhere, with certificates from Let's Encrypt and HTTP redirected;
- nightly encrypted database backups, with a copy off the server recommended (#37), plus a documented restore that's tested at least once before launch;
- the database reachable only from the app;
- `APP_URL` set to the site's public address (for example `https://ssvs.example.org`), because every link the app builds, in emails and on screens, uses it and never the address a request was sent to;
- nginx answering requests for any other host name with an empty reply (a `default_server` block that returns 444), so only the site's own address reaches the app. The app refuses other host names too, as a second layer.
- `TRUSTED_PROXIES` left empty for the standard AlmaLinux layout, nginx talking to PHP-FPM over FastCGI. That layout has no proxy: PHP already gets the visitor's address and whether the connection is HTTPS from the parameters nginx passes. The setting is only for an HTTP proxy or load balancer in front of the app (nginx using `proxy_pass` to a separate PHP app server, Caddy, HAProxy, a cloud load balancer). In that case set it to the proxy's address (a comma-separated list, for example 127.0.0.1 when the proxy runs on the same server) and make the proxy send the `X-Forwarded-For` and `X-Forwarded-Proto` headers, so the app believes the visitor address and scheme they report. `*` trusts every sender, must be the whole value, and is only for an app that can be reached only through the proxy. Left empty, those headers are ignored.

### People leaving

When someone stops volunteering, a coordinator turns their account off. That removes them from every future shift (cancelled ones included) and ends each of their regular slots yesterday, in the same click and without emailing them, because the person is leaving and the people running the schedule asked for it. The Turn off section on their page says how many shifts and slots that is before the button. Shifts that have started keep them, so history stays. Turning an account back on doesn't put them back on anything. Removing a person does the same first: their schedule is released and committed, and then the removal happens, so if the removal is then refused their schedule stays cleared. People turned off before this change were left on their future shifts; turning them off again tidies that, and there's no button for it yet. Leaving a lead or coordinator role removes that access immediately. Member sync, where configured, deactivates accounts for people who leave the organization.

## 15. Technical approach

- **One install per organization.** No multi-tenancy.
- **Server:** AlmaLinux.
- **Language and framework:** PHP with Laravel, starting on Laravel 13 (PHP 8.3 or newer, which AlmaLinux 10 ships by default). Laravel's notification system already covers mail, SMS, and webhook channels, it has a built-in scheduler and job queue for generation, escalation, and sync jobs, and its Fortify package handles login, two-factor authentication and rate limiting.
- **Screens:** Blade, Laravel's built-in server-rendered templates, with as little JavaScript as possible. It has the fewest dependencies to upgrade, works well on phones, and makes accessible markup the default. Styling is one plain CSS file with no build step and no Node on the server, and the font (Atkinson Hyperlegible, as in the wireframes) is served from the install itself, so no outside font service sees who's using the app. Scripts live in files under `public/js` and are loaded with `<script src defer>`; views never contain inline scripts, event handler attributes (`onchange=` and the like) or `<style>` elements, because the Content-Security-Policy (section 13) doesn't allow them. Every screen still works with JavaScript off.
- **Tests:** PHPUnit, Laravel's default test framework, run against MariaDB rather than SQLite so date and time handling matches production. Behavior that needs JavaScript, which PHPUnit can't run, is tested in a real browser with Laravel Dusk, Laravel's first-party browser testing package, against Chromium in its own container, so no Node is needed anywhere (decision log, 2026-11-15).
- **Static analysis and dependency checks:** Larastan (PHPStan with Laravel's rules), a development-only package, runs in CI at level 5 over `app/` against a baseline of the errors that existed when it was added, so a new error fails the build and the baseline can shrink over time. `composer audit --locked` runs in CI too and fails on a known security advisory in any locked package (decision log, 2026-11-25).
- **Planning for upgrades:** Laravel releases a major version about once a year, and each gets security fixes for two years, so every install will face a major upgrade roughly yearly. Organizations run SSVS with volunteer maintainers, so upgrades have to stay small and safe:
  - Stay close to framework defaults and documented conventions. Avoid overriding framework internals.
  - Add third-party packages only when they save real work, prefer first-party Laravel packages, and record why each one was added.
  - Keep automated tests on every core flow (section 8) and on the access rules (section 13), so an upgrade can be checked by running the tests.
  - Move SSVS to each new Laravel major within six months of its release, and note the required PHP version and any server changes in the release notes.
  - Run automated dependency checks so vulnerable packages show up as pull requests.
- **Database:** MariaDB.
- **Deployment:** a single server behind nginx, optionally in Docker. On an update, migrations run before the scheduler resumes, because every email send reads the `email_wordings` table and fails if the code is newer than the database. SSVS sends email as it goes and queues nothing, so there's no queue worker. Nightly encrypted database backups, with a copy off the server recommended (#37), plus the host's snapshot backups. The steps are in docs/install.md, and `deploy/test/smoke.sh` runs them in a container.
- **Health check and destructive commands:** `/up` is for monitors. It's a plain page of our own (`routes/health.php`, answering `OK` as plain text, or 500 if something listening to Laravel's health event fails), outside the web group so it sets no session cookie, and it keeps answering during maintenance mode, with or without cached routes (the exemption is set with the middleware in `bootstrap/app.php`). `migrate:fresh`, `migrate:refresh`, `migrate:reset` and `db:wipe` delete every table, so they refuse to run on a real server, with or without `--force`. They run only when `APP_ENV` is `local` or `testing`. Restoring a backup on a real server never uses them: drop and recreate the database with the `mariadb` client and import the dump, which is what `ssvs-restore` does (docs/install.md, section 9).
- **Production settings:** a production `.env` sets `APP_ENV=production`, `APP_DEBUG=false`, `LOG_LEVEL=warning`, `SESSION_SECURE_COOKIE=true` and a real `MAIL_MAILER`; `.env.example` is for development and says so at the top. The app enforces the debug and mailer rules itself at start-up (section 13).
- **License:** AGPL-3.0, so anyone who runs a modified version as a service shares their changes.

## 16. Phases

**Phase 1: run the front desk.**
Invite-only accounts with password and email-link login, 2FA for admins, coordinators and area leads. Roles. Areas. Weekly slots (every week and every other week) with regulars. One-time shifts. Overlapping shifts. Closures. Taking, releasing, and take links. Cover requests and the full escalation ladder. Editing with "only this date" or "all following", followed by change notices. Shadow spots, clearance, the required-shadow setting, and moving back. Rotation sign-up. Email and chat notifications. Per-person notification settings. The coordinator week view and "needs attention" list. The security and privacy requirements in section 13, including SECURITY.md and a security review before go-live.

**Phase 2: visibility.**
Events with organizers. Public calendar. Google Calendar publishing and personal iCal feeds. Member directory sync.

**Phase 3: polish.**
Hours export. Direct person-to-person swaps. Staffing reports. Skills on event shifts. Text messages, once a provider is chosen and registered.

**Phase 4: hours and credit.**
Volunteer hours (worked shifts, no-shows and each person's totals), credit requests and the review queue (section 8.6). Adding approved credit to the membership system automatically comes after that.

## 17. Launch approach

Run a test copy a couple of weeks before launch. Coordinators enter the real schedule there, confirming each regular as they go, which doubles as their training and surfaces confusing screens while they're cheap to fix. Load the volunteer list into the test copy with `ssvs:import-people`, which adds people as not invited yet. Copy the whole test database to the live install, which keeps the schedule, the people and the change log together, use the test copy's `APP_KEY` in the live install's `.env` (Fortify encrypts two-factor secrets and recovery codes with it, so a different key breaks every coordinator's authenticator). Copy the live install's `.env` to the off-server copy and make a live backup, then shut the test copy down and delete its database and `.env`, so the key that protects two-factor secrets lives only on the live install and in the off-server copy of its `.env`. After that, press "Invite everyone not invited yet" on the people list, so on day one every volunteer logs in and finds their shifts already there. Don't invite volunteers from the test copy. Don't auto-import an old shared calendar: they tend to carry stale recurring entries and inconsistent names.

A failure before the database is dropped says nothing in the database was changed and brings the site back up; a failure after the drop but before the backup is in names the rollback backup; a failure after the backup is in says it's restored, lists the steps left to finish by hand, and says so if the scheduler was off before the restore and is still off. |
